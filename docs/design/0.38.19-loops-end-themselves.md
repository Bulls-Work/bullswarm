# Review loops that end by themselves (eval 2026-10-31, top-5 #4)

Status: chosen option C, behind the flag `workflow goal --out-of-rounds end`
(default `wait`, today's behaviour). Order bs-eval-followups.

## The problem, measured

In the 14 days to 2026-10-31T04:46Z, 47 dry review loops ran
(`until: {step, dry}`). Of the 33 that ended:

| how it ended | loops |
|---|---|
| dry (every area reviewed clean after the last finding) | 10 |
| low yield (the 0.38.17 rule) | 7 |
| out of rounds, then ended by a person with `workflow continue` | **16** |

(The other 14 were blocked by a failed step or are still pending.)

What the person did at each of the 16 (from `continue-<loop>.json` and the
`workflow.waiting` event of each run):

- **16 of 16 ended the loop** (`continue` without `--rounds`, recorded
  `continued-unmet`). Nobody ever added rounds.
- The loop waited a **median of 0.4 min**, longest 3.8 min, 11 min in all.
  The "person" was an orchestrating agent that answered the wake and always
  chose the same option.
- All 16 runs then completed.

So the wake carries no decision today: it costs a turn of the caller and,
in the benchmark, counts as a human intervention (t2 and t4 on 2026-10-31).

## Options

Each option was replayed over the 33 ended loops with
`bench/loop-stop-2026-10-31/analyze.py` + `sim.py` (method below). "Rounds not run"
and "findings those rounds found" count rounds a rule would have skipped
compared with what really ran; those findings were real in-scope findings
at or above the loop's floor, which the skipped rounds would not have fixed.
Cost per round is the loop's API-equivalent spend divided by its rounds
(inferred, not measured per round).

| option | of the 16, ends by itself | still need a person | rounds not run | ~$ not spent | findings those rounds found |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| today | 0 | 16 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| A. severity floor raised to `everyday` | 3 | 13 | 7 | 39 | 13 |
| B1. dry rounds: credit an area whose latest review was clean | 2 | 14 | 1 | 3 | 0 |
| B2. dry rounds: one clean round is enough (no area coverage) | 4 | 12 | 10 | 34 | 3 |
| C. budget: the declared `maxRounds` is the budget; out of rounds ends the loop | **16** | **0** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| C'. budget cap $20 per loop | 4 | 12 | 12 | 108 | 50 |
| C'. budget cap $15 per loop | 8 | 8 | 25 | 190 | 77 |
| C'. budget cap $10 per loop | 14 | 2 | 43 | 272 | 117 |

Why the loops did not run dry (the 16, by hand):

- 4 had a clean last round but an area not re-reviewed since the last
  finding (ie4iis, i2g7p2, 2eq4c2, 5j3hps).
- 2 found nothing new in the last round, but a fix had not held (a known
  finding reported `stillOpen`: mizewi, u4hd22).
- 10 were still finding new fix-worthy bugs in the last round (1-9 new).
  They were not converging; more rounds would have found more.
- 5 of the 16 had `maxRounds` 2 with 4 areas and 3 reviewers a round:
  covering every area takes 2 rounds, so after any finding in round 1 they
  could not end dry (the `dry-loop-too-short` advisory says so at validate).

## Choice: C, the declared rounds are the budget

- **A** drops real bugs (13 in-scope findings) and overrides a floor the
  author chose; it rescues 3 of 16.
- **B1** is safe (0 findings lost) but rescues 2 of 16. **B2** rescues 4 and
  lets a round that skipped an area count as proof for it.
- **A money cap (C')** stops loops that were still finding bugs: at $15 it
  rescues half and skips rounds that found 77 real findings. Spend per
  review is the separate cost item on the backlog.
- **C** removes all 16 wakes and changes no finding, no round and no cost:
  it does at once what every caller did by hand. The author already states
  the budget as `maxRounds`; C makes that the end.

What C keeps honest: the loop still records that its condition did not
hold (`rounds-spent`, read as condition not met exactly like
`continued-unmet`), the run's proof line and the Run page say so, and the
findings summary still lists what was found and what was not re-reviewed.
Steps behind the loop run as after a `continue`.

Behind a flag because ending is a change of contract for a caller who reads
the wake as a chance to add rounds: `workflow goal --out-of-rounds
end|wait`, default `wait`. Kept in the run's config, so `resume` keeps it.
If the next eval shows no caller adding rounds, flip the default.

B1 is a cheap, safe refinement of the dry rule that could ship on its own
later; it does not change this choice.

## Method

- `analyze.py` reads every `~/.bullswarm/workflows/*/state.json` whose run
  started in the window, takes each dry loop's record (`controlNodes`, type
  loop, with `findings`), its declared loop (`program.control.loops`), its
  area reviewers (`program.actions[].areaOf`) and the API-equivalent spend of
  the loop's attempts.
- `sim.py` replays each stop rule round by round over the recorded rounds:
  fix-worthy = new + reopened (for A: `everyday` new + reopened only);
  an area's findings come from the known list's `area` and `reopened`
  rounds. A rule that would stop a dry or low-yield loop later than it really
  stopped keeps the real stop.
- Person's choice and wait: the loop's `continue-<loop>.json` (`rounds`
  null = ended) against the `since` of its `workflow.waiting` entry.

## Benchmark check

Re-run t2 (review loop) and t4 (minisql) of `bench/eval-suite` with
`--goal-flags "--out-of-rounds end"`: expected 0 interventions, hidden-test
scores comparable to 32/32 and 141/141.
