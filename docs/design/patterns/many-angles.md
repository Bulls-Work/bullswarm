# Many angles

**Reach for it when:**
- the question has no single right lens;
- being wrong is costly;
- one agent keeps giving the same answer.

Typical questions: "what are the risks of X", "should we do Y or Z", "what
does the evidence say about W".

**Shape**

```
angle-1 ┐
angle-2 ├→ compare → [check] → the caller or the person decides, between runs
angle-3 ┘
```

**Steps**
- **angle-N**
  - Role: investigate. Deliverable: a report.
  - Each angle gets one lens: customers, cost, risk, precedent, or a
    skeptic.
  - Ask the same question every time, with a different brief per angle.
  - Ask for a verdict first, and a source or quoted observation for each
    claim.
- **compare**
  - Role: combine. Deliverable: a report.
  - It lists where the angles agree, where they conflict, and what would
    settle each conflict.
  - It does not pick a winner.
- **check** (optional)
  - Role: check. Deliverable: a report.
  - It opens a sample of the cited sources and marks claims that do not hold.

**The caller decides:**
- which angles (3–5 is plenty);
- whether to route angles to different providers;
- what to do with the comparison. The decision itself is not a step.

**What backs "done":**
- review: the check step, or the compare step tracing each claim it uses to
  a cited source;
- choice: the decision, made between runs.

**More than one provider:** helps a lot. Different vendors give genuinely
different takes. On one subscription, vary the brief and the reasoning level
instead.

**Pitfalls**
- Angles that are really one lens, reworded.
- A compare step that votes instead of mapping conflicts.
- Using many angles for a question a single study would answer.
- Letting the compare step make the decision that belongs to the person.

**Neighbors:** argue-both-sides (two opposed angles) · multi-source-research
(the angles are sources) · explore-decide-build (what usually follows).
